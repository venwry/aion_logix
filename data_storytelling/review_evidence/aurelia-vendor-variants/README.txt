COOPERATION_MODEL
Third party owns, manufactures, brands, sells and supports the finished compatible offering. Logix may assist design and AION Labs may supply chips/ROMs. OEM-only supply to Logix and Logix-owned/resold products do not qualify.

PROBABILITY_MEANING
Subjective range for vendor commitment to the specified project within roughly 18–24 months of the May 1979 independent programme, under strong Aurelia sales and full interface support. Not historical frequencies, statistical confidence intervals or per-model conversion odds inferred from a manufacturer-level probability.

THRESHOLD
Arithmetic midpoint (low + high) / 2 >= 70; inclusive. The author explicitly directs treating qualifying projects as supplied in the scenario. The interval endpoints and midpoint are retained without rounding for selection.

RELEASE_POLICY
Estimated month for the scenario offering, after the May 1979 programme and after the source technology is commercially available. Standard-interface projects need about 6–12 months; complete storage/specialist redesigns about 11–18 months. Dates can extend into 1980; no assumption that all variants shipped by 1979.

HISTORY_BOUNDARY
The historical 200-item catalog and 12 preexisting records are not rewritten as fictional historical facts. Forty scenario offerings receive distinct IDs and dates; source records receive only a descriptive link. Shared Centronics, Hayes, Morrow, Percom and Computalker projects are not multiplied into one product per historical source.

STANDARDS_BOUNDARY
Existing printer/disk/keyboard classes are reused. Modem, cartridge, plotter, audio and other specialist functions need vendor protocols and qualified drivers over the existing transport; no universal profile or later pointer/network feature is backdated.

SOURCE_LIMITS
Historical identities and features use the existing catalog and its source index. Probability ranges, ownership arrangements and release dates are authored counterfactual judgments following the conversation, not claims found in those historical sources.

SCOPE
All 201 current peripheral records and all 68 provider actors are assessed. The original 200 peripheral entries plus the already-established Model II FlexiBUS host option account for this total. Computer and software records are outside this task.

IMPORTANT REVISIONS FROM THE CHAT ESTIMATES
The earlier estimates mixed independent branded products, OEM supply and terminal-derived assemblies. Only vendor-owned/vendor-sold products now qualify.
Shugart: 25–45% (35% midpoint); Micropolis: 45–65% (55%); PerSci: 55–70% (62.5%). Their previously favored bare-mechanism/OEM arrangements do not qualify.
Matrox: 45–65% (55%); terminal suppliers: below 70%, because OEM graphics/display/keyboard assemblies and awkward console replacement no longer count.
TI: 60–75% (67.5%); North Star: 55–75% (65%); environmental image capture: 50–70% (60%). No assumed version is added.
Sanyo: 65–80% (72.5%); Leedex: 75–90% (82.5%). Both qualify as independently sold monitor variants, not monitor supply contracts to Logix.
Centronics: 85–95% (90%). One independently sold bridge serves two historical printer records; neither printer becomes a native FlexiBUS device.
The Micro Works, Heath, SWTPC and Smoke Signal each reach the inclusive threshold at exactly 70%. The selected programmer/printer/disk project is included; other products are assessed separately.
Manufacturer probability is for at least one qualifying project. It is not copied onto every product in the manufacturer catalog. Product-specific ranges and exclusions are retained in assessment.json.

MODEL AND REVIEW NOTES
Original historical release fields and all original impact assessments are preserved. Each existing record gains one descriptive cross-reference to the separate scenario offering; no historical identity is silently renamed.
New offerings use direct release dates at month precision, provider_actor_ids naming only the vendor, and scoped impacted_standards / impacted_products. Probability and selection metadata remain in editorial evidence.
Vendor ownership of the finished product does not transfer AION ownership of its chip designs or make Logix the seller. No Logix product, device, actor, agreement, campaign or manufacturing recipe is created.
Native printer variants can compete with FlexiPrint40 while implementing FlexiBUS. Bridge relationships support original printers without claiming native FlexiBUS conformance.
Missing specialist core classes are not invented as already released standards: the scenario offerings use documented vendor protocols and qualified Aurelia drivers over the existing transport. Universal class governance, measured conformance and semiconductor process evidence are not fabricated.
Historical sources remain indexed in ../external-peripherals-1979/catalog.json; source IDs in offerings.json point into that catalog. They support historical product features, not the scenario probabilities or launch dates.
