# NCC 1979 content review

> **Historical documentation — not current rules, instructions or queue status.** Preserved from main at `606e3f1`; consult the [current documentation index](../README.md) and live storytelling JSON.

Seven single-record proposals revise the campaign, execution process and launch event, retire the duplicate Apple launch event, and update its three referencing records. These are pending author approval, not edits to approved foundation data.

The existing schema is sufficient. Marketing-content types now describe useful materials rather than flattened field paths. Dates, compatibility facts and forecasts appear as complete sentences rather than bare values such as `false`, `1979-06-04` or `printers`. Venue and demonstration guidance move out of the timed presentation stages. The eight timed stages still total 50 minutes, excluding questions.

The rewrite preserves independent licensing, native Apple and S-100 distinctions, initial AION chip supply, optional display adoption, qualified prototype status, the 50 KB/s FlexiBUS ceiling, display-resolution limits, migration work and the architecture embargo through 19 May 1980. The future USD 100 price outlook remains Adam’s qualified forecast, including its approximate horizon, exclusions and TV-interface engineering gap.

Recruitment priorities and qualitative manufacturer assessments now guide process actions. The campaign retains the adoption targets and clearly labels them as targets. Metrics and evidence collection belong to execution; process outcomes do not claim that recruitment or sales targets were achieved.

## Event consolidation

`event.apple_extensions` merges into `event.ncc79_campaign`, preserving its five products, Apple participant, initial chip supply, native drivers/S-100 adapters and `agreement.apple_cooperation`. The agreement applies to the Apple cooperation component, not to every NCC participant. Apple does not endorse S-100 systems.

The campaign execution process remains the merged event’s sole timeline parent. The development envelope loses the separate expansion-launch step. Apple’s compatibility-review event and market-response record refer to the merged event. Approve the complete pack together, or apply the surviving records and reference changes before deleting the old event.
